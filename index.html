By the time I started, there wasn’t a separate Deaf camp session. It was just part of the centre’s mindset and culture have any accessibility for mindset and culture benefit at everyone not just the deaf part of hearing, and CODA kids

SLIDE 06 - 1.2 PESO MODEL

There's an entire podcast, book, and a marketing agency based on the PESO model.

The PESO Model (Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned) was created and coined by Gini Dietrich, founder of the marketing blog and agency, and podcast of the same name "Spin Sucks,"

introduced in her 2014 book "Spin Sucks: Communication and Reputation Management in the Digital Age"

It structures modern marketing into four CATegories:

Four cats on this slide. I'm going through them in clockwise order from 10 o'clock.

  1. PAID: Cat with one blue eye and one green eye behind a large wallet full of cash: ->paid ads --> "Boosting" a post or running an ad, or printing materials for a conference. Any marketing that you have to pay to distribute.
  2. EARNED: The A11y Cat on a tv with tv antennas. -> when some one features you on their social or other media
  3. SHARED: Cat with a laptop in front facing us. The screen has a YouTube play button on it. -> What you post on socials. YouTube videos, Instagram posts/reels, articles on SubStack, AND chats in dark socials like Slack and Discord, And GitHub.
  4. OWNED: Cat laying down on two records. -> What you actually have on your own websites (not Medium), or backed up on your own Hard drives, or in print.
  • Why? Platforms can shut down and change the rules at any time. There's a YouTuber that makes shorter long form cat videos that was demonetized for "inauthentic content". to integrate media types rather than keeping them siloed.
  • Not just social media. It applies to all media in this digital age:

SLIDE 14 - 2.4 Alt Text vs Image Description

Three cats in front of two versions of Van Goth's Stary night.

Even though it's the same content - starry night - they have a different design style and feeling

Alt Text (Alternative Text)Purpose:

  • Provides a concise, functional alternative for visually impaired users and displays if an image fails to load.
  • Visibility: Hidden within HTML code or image metadata.
  • Length: Short (ideally under 125–150 characters).
  • Content: Focuses on the "what" and "why" (e.g., "Company logo," "A black cat sitting on a fence").

Image Description (Extended/Long Description)

  • Purpose: Offers detailed, contextual, or emotional information, making complex visuals (like graphs) accessible.
  • Visibility: Visible to all users (e.g., in a caption, or below the photo with "Image Description:" or "ID:").
  • Length: Variable—can be several sentences or a paragraph.
  • Content: Detailed, covering visual nuances like colours, shapes, and complex data points.

More Stats:

  • On-Screen Presence: Characters with disabilities account for roughly 2.4% to 2.8% of speaking or named characters in mainstream film and media.

SLIDE 21 - 3.3 Pay Disabled People!

Have you as an arts organization and/or artist been asked to work for free because "It's good exposure."?

It's like those companies and people are on a different planet.

It's such a common occurrence for disabled people too.

Even Molly Burke, author, a "Forbes 30 Under 30", C-Corp Founder and owner, public speaker, and OG YouTube creator gets these requests every week because she's blind.

Exposure doesn't pay the bills. Asking disabled people, artists, and/or disabled artists to work for free undermines the value of our work, our professions.

If you're on a tight budget, look at ways to equitably compensate disabled people for our time, expertise and experience.

E.g. AMI paid me a $25 honourarium to fill out a longer online survey. They have a pool of disabled people on hand to do so.

HIRE disabled neurodivergent, and Deaf people. Make your hiring process and workplace more accessible, and not just for us wheelchair users (though that's obviously important).

Think about neurodivergent accessibility, Deaf/HoH, chronically ill, blind applicants in all parts of your hiring and call for proposals process.

Reach out to organizations that support the disability community like the CNIB (Canadian National Institute for the Blind), Spinal Cord Injury Ontario, The Canadian Hearing Society.

Most offer free or low cost job posting services.

SLIDE 23 - 3.4 Avoid These Three Things

  • Ablism
  • Disability Tropes
  • Inspiration Porn

The three things you should avoid both in your social media and life.

  1. Ableism is discrimination and social prejudice against people with disabilities, based on the belief that typical abilities are superior.

That woman wouldn't have congraduatled a non-disabled person for crossing the street.

  • That would have been werid.

  • But she sees wheelchair users as less capable, so OF COURSE she congradulated me on crossing the street.

  • Disability Tropes and common misbeliefs things like

  • the disabled villian
  • the poor pitiful disabled person

  • That we want a miracle cure The woman in my story saw me as the poor pitiful disabled person who should be congratulated for crossing the street.

  • and of course the "Super Crip/Super hero"
